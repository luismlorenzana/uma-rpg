1. The cell and its anchors

Each frame is 64×64. The Uma stands about 52 px tall, the study's 48×64 size. Her feet sit on the ground line y = 60, centred on x = 32, and the game places every frame by that point. She faces image-left in three-quarter view and is never mirrored.

AnchorWhat it is
foot(32, 60), the same in every frame
head boxHead, ears and front hair; must match the key frame on head-locked frames
face boxEyes and mouth; the face patches are its size
grip + angleFront hand; 0° = forward (image-left), 90° = up
offBack hand: a tome, a bow string
hitWhere blows aimed at her land

4. The face layer

On head-locked frames (idle, walk, run, shoot, cast, guard) the head is the key frame's, only moved. The game stamps a face patch on them and runs the blinks. On free frames (swing, thrust, hop, the fall, victory) the face is drawn in. The lock tolerates 6 changed pixels outside the face box, and also guards her size and identity.

Blinks: every 2.4–5 s at random, half 60 ms, closed 100 ms, half 60 ms; one in seven doubled.

5. The landscape fight (the turned stage)

844×390 pt. Cells at 4 device px a pixel: 85 pt square, the Uma about 69 pt tall. Foes take the left ~55%; the party stands in two staggered columns on the right, three at the back and two in front. HP, MP and Limit sit in a party strip; commands are at the bottom right, in thumb reach.

6. HD-2D, layer by layer

An orthographic camera tilted down keeps every sprite at a whole number of device pixels; depth of field gives the depth. three.js draws the fight; the DOM keeps the numbers and controls (HD2–HD4).
