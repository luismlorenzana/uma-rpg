The world: a Hub, five regions, five bands

Each region is a Way's homeland. Going outward, each ring is a band of ten levels. Every zone has a persisted town and two dungeons, marked with their suggested levels: an easier and a harder one. Hover a zone for its details.

25 towns, built by hand from kits, and permanent. A chapter stages what it needs while it plays (a broken bridge, who stands where) and leaves no lasting change, so chapters play in any order and each outfit's replay of a story finds the town as it expects.
50 dungeons, ten a band, one at each suggested level. DN21's ten weapon kinds pair with each band's ten.
Five hand-made dungeons, one at each region's edge (ringed). The other 45 are generated from seeds.
Chapter 1 starts in her Way's inner town, so a party's five chapter 1s tour all five regions (the dashed line).
The map is a screen, not a walked field. Tap a place next to one you've reached; anywhere you've reached is one tap away.
The Hub is a town too, with the jobs, the workbench and storage. Travelers in no party live there.

How a chapter plays

A chapter runs only when its traveler leads. 20–40 minutes; the slice (Q12, Q13) measures it.

The quest logShows each member's next chapter, its town and its suggested level. Never locked.
Opening scenesIn the town: the speakers walk, emote and talk, with master data's portraits by the text box.
TravelOn the world map to the chapter's dungeon, one of the band's ten.
The dungeonRandom battles, Springs, waystones, HP carried: the DN calls.
The boss roomHer chapter's boss: a rival Uma with escorts, or a bestiary monster with its own script.
Closing scenesBack in town. Flags change the town; her next chapter appears in the next band.

The party menu

At any town's inn. One tab for each Way, 20 travelers in each. Every outfit is its own traveler with her own level, gear and story progress; outfits of one character share her story, and a party never holds two of them. The menu fills one of each Way by default and warns without a Healer, but never forbids a mix.

Five stories out-level a band

A party's five chapter Ns and their dungeons take it through band N, about two levels a chapter. The order is the player's; here it runs easiest dungeon first. After all five, the party is past the band's hardest dungeon.

The curve is set in Phase 4 from the slice's measured minutes and fights (CM30). 25 chapters take a party from Lv 1 to 50; each new party walks the same dungeons and meets its own leads' bosses.

One dungeon, many bosses

Every dungeon has a resident boss. When the lead's chapter is set there, her chapter's boss takes the room for that run, and the drops stay the dungeon's. With 65 chapter 1s across band 1's ten dungeons, each dungeon holds about six or seven story bosses.

Rival another traveler and her escorts, on her own sprites. She can't fight her own party: the chapter waits while any of her outfits travels with the lead.

Bestiary a monster on one of about six large bodies a band, with its own name, colours, skills and fight script.

Where the stories come from

Each traveler's story sets a public-domain tale's shape over her real racehorse's career. We use plots only, from sources that are anonymous and old, or by authors who died more than 70 years ago, published before 1931, and every line is written fresh. Never a modern RPG's story, and never Uma Musume's career scripts. Stories are private content and never go in git.

No traveler dies or is maimed in her story: a real horse's fatal or career-ending injury becomes a trial she survives (CM13). Several travelers can share a family; no two share a tale.

Waves and volume

Stories ship five travelers at a time, one of each Way, and each wave is a full run of 25 chapters. Wave 1 is Phases 5 and 6; waves 2–20 are Phase 7, in your tier order. A wave writes only the characters not yet written, so the later waves are mostly replays of finished stories.
